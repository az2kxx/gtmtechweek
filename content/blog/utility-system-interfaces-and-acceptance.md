# Utility system upgrades: define the acceptance boundary before choosing suppliers

DRAFT — English; global utility/integration audience; reviewed 7 September 2026. General guidance, not an announcement of a team, award or commercial mandate.

A useful procurement brief should explain who is accountable for a working system, not only list the components being bought. That distinction matters when a project spans equipment, software and site work.

## Separate the installed estate from the new purchase

Cairns’ Contract 3047 names a migration from Citect SCADA to AVEVA Plant SCADA. That establishes a software transition, but it does not by itself establish a server-replacement requirement. A scope review should first identify existing assets, licence rights, interfaces and what the buyer actually requires to change. [Cairns council notice](https://www.cairns.qld.gov.au/council/tenders/open-tenders/3047)

## Make interfaces into reviewable work packages

For a gas-regulating station, component compliance alone does not explain who owns installation, metering integration, testing and handover. PGCL’s 55 MMSCFD invitation combines design, supply and installation, illustrating why those boundaries belong in the qualification review. The full specifications—not a generic equipment list—must determine the final allocation. [Official PGCL invitation](https://www.bppa.gov.bd/advertisement-goods/details-98305.html)

A practical scoping matrix can list each interface, the evidence needed, its proposed owner, acceptance criteria and unresolved dependencies. These are recommended review fields, not claims about a buyer’s undisclosed requirements.

## Start with one accountable provider

If one eligible company can evidence the whole scope, a second company is unnecessary. A complementary team is useful when it closes a specific capability or eligibility gap and can assign responsibility clearly. A proposed role is not evidence of willingness, capacity or commitment.

## Check access before investing in a bid

A closing date may come after document-purchase, registration or compulsory-briefing gates. The relevant question is therefore not only whether a tender remains listed, but whether a particular bidder can still participate. Unknown gates need review; missed mandatory gates are not fixed by optimistic outreach.

## Keep the commercial proposition separate

The integrator’s proposed service can be opportunity qualification and technical coordination. Its payer, fee, scope and payment trigger must be agreed independently of the tender’s headline value. Contract ceilings, qualification thresholds and security deposits are not the integrator’s earnings.
